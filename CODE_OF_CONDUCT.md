# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

TEOS is committed to a respectful, inclusive, accessible, and professional
community. Contributors should communicate constructively, welcome different
experience levels and perspectives, give specific technical feedback, accept
review gracefully, respect boundaries, and prioritize learner safety and
privacy.

Examples of unacceptable behavior include harassment, discrimination, threats,
sexualized attention, personal attacks, stalking, doxing, deliberate
disruption, sustained bad-faith argument, retaliation, and disclosure of
another person's private information.

Good-faith criticism of decisions and maintainers is welcome. Disagreement,
mistakes, and requests for evidence are not misconduct by themselves.

## Scope

This standard applies in repository discussions, issues, pull requests,
reviews, project events and community spaces, and when someone publicly
represents the project. It also applies to private conduct that creates a
credible safety risk for project participants.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behavior privately to a maintainer through the contact
method identified in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). State that the report concerns
conduct, include relevant links or records, describe the impact and immediate
safety needs, and name any person who should not receive the report because of
a conflict.

Do not submit conduct reports as public issues. Maintainers will protect the
reporter's identity and details as far as practical, but may need to share a
limited account to investigate or act. Knowingly false reports and retaliation
are violations; an unsubstantiated good-faith report is not.

## Enforcement

Maintainers consider context, impact, history, power imbalance, safety, and the
wishes of affected people. Proportionate actions may include:

1. a private clarification or correction;
2. a warning and required change in behavior;
3. removal or editing of content;
4. temporary participation restrictions;
5. removal of project privileges; or
6. a permanent ban.

Immediate restrictions may be used to protect people or evidence while a
report is assessed. Enforcement decisions should identify the policy violated,
scope, duration, and conditions for return when safe to do so.

A maintainer named in or conflicted by a report must recuse themselves.
Unconflicted maintainers decide the response. When only one maintainer exists
and is conflicted, the project should seek a trusted independent open-source
community moderator while taking only the temporary steps necessary for
safety.

Anyone subject to a non-emergency enforcement action may request one
reconsideration by an unconflicted maintainer within 30 days. Appeals address
process, new evidence, proportionality, or conflict; they are not an
opportunity to contact or pressure the reporter.
